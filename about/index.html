St. Thomas Duct Cleaning: Planning a duct cleaning Job

For a commercial duct cleaning project in St. Thomas, gather the building type, occupied hours, ventilation equipment list, and any access or security requirements before requesting a scope. Offices, retail spaces, daycares, and workshops can have different duct layouts and rooftop equipment, so ask what systems and areas are included. Has airflow from the system changed?

A furnace and AC cleaning project in St. Thomas may involve more than the ductwork. If the system has had an equipment conversion, replacement, or repair, note the make and model and photograph the accessible blower and coil areas. Ask which components are included, whether the blower assembly will be removed, and how an evaporator coil and condensate drain will be handled. The equipment configuration and access determine the appropriate scope; cleaning is distinct from equipment repair or performance testing.

Are ventilation fans running without clearing the air?

For HRV and ventilation cleaning in St. Thomas, identify each component you want included: the HRV core, screens, condensate paths, bathroom exhaust ducts, or range hood run. If the home is tightly sealed or has been renovated, note how the ventilation equipment is configured and whether fans seem to move air. Photograph accessible labels and grilles, then ask what can be serviced safely and what access is required. Component-specific details affect the scope and help separate cleaning from repairs.

Does an odour return when the system starts?

If you are considering duct sanitizing or deodorizing in St. Thomas, first describe the odour, when it occurs, and whether it began after smoke exposure, pets, a period with the system unused, or a change in occupancy. Ask what cleaning must happen before any treatment and which surfaces are included. A treatment does not identify or remove every odour source, and it should not be presented as a health measure. Request product details and label-based use information before deciding.

  • The work you need

    Compare whole-system supply and return duct scopes. Note older, modified, or fragile runs and ask how access and cleaning methods will be adapted and documented. Describe the route from dryer to exterior hood, including rises, elbows, and concealed runs. Ask whether full-length lint removal and an airflow check are included. Ask whether the proposed scope includes blower assembly removal, evaporator coil washing, and condensate drain clearing. Equipment configuration affects access and method.

  • The wider system

    Identify HRV cores, screens, bathroom exhaust runs, and range hood ducts separately. Confirm which components and exterior terminations are included. Describe the odour and its likely context before considering treatment. Ask what cleaning comes first and request product and label-use details.

    Photograph gaps, crushed sections, and modified runs. Ask how leaks will be located and whether repairs include sealing or replacement of damaged ductwork.
